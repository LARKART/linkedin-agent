## Caption

OpenAI's new 'Dots' agents got a lot of attention this week for one reason: they keep working after you close your laptop.

That's the headline. It's not really the interesting part.

To act while you're offline, an agent needs somewhere to act from. Its own cloud computer. Its own browser. Its own logins and permissions. That's a very different thing than a chatbot answering questions in a window.

OpenAI's own setup makes this clear. Reading things is automatic. Sending, editing, or touching a password requires a human to approve it first. Custom rules let you mark any action as allow, approve, or block.

That's not a limitation of the model. That's the actual engineering problem.

A public demo this week didn't go as planned, and a lot of the discussion since has been about reliability. But reliability was never going to come from a smarter model alone.

It comes from the structure around it. What the agent can touch. What it has to ask permission for. What never gets handed over at all.

For any business looking at agents right now, the useful question isn't "can it do the task." It's "what happens the one time it gets the task wrong, and who's watching for that."

Demo-ready and business-ready are answered by two different checklists.

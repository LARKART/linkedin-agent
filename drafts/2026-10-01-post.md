## Caption

There's a detail in the OpenAI Dots launch that matters more than the chatbot interface.

Dots gives an agent its own compute and its own browser. It can keep working between conversations, not just respond inside one.

That's a different question than "what can this answer."

It's "what can this own without me watching it."

Businesses have been quietly wrestling with that question for years, just under a different name: automation.

Which steps can run unattended. Which ones need a human to check before anything moves forward. Where does a retry happen automatically, and where does a mistake actually cost money or trust.

A persistent agent doesn't remove that design problem. It just makes it more visible, because now the agent is running when nobody's looking at the screen.

The interesting part isn't that the model got smarter.

It's that the industry is finally building infrastructure around the same guardrails automation teams have needed all along: ownership boundaries, monitoring, and a clear answer to what happens when something goes wrong while no one's watching.

What would you actually trust an agent to own in your business without supervision right now?

## Caption

There's a shift happening in how AI agents get deployed, and it's worth paying attention to.

For the last two years, most AI tools have been on-demand. You open a chat window, ask a question, get an answer, and the agent goes back to sleep.

The conversation around OpenAI's Dots and ChatGPT's work-focused agents points somewhere different: agents that stay active across Slack, email, GitHub, and the browser, working while you're not watching.

That's a meaningful change, not a feature update.

An on-demand chatbot only needs human-in-the-loop at the moment you're using it. An always-on agent needs it designed into the system itself: what it's allowed to touch, when it escalates, what gets logged, what requires approval before it goes out the door.

Most businesses experimenting with agents right now are still thinking in chatbot terms. Ask a question, review the answer, move on.

An agent that reads your inbox, opens pull requests, or posts in Slack overnight needs guardrails before it needs intelligence. The interesting engineering problem isn't making the agent smarter. It's deciding where it's allowed to act without you, and building the checkpoints for everywhere else.

If you're building or evaluating agent systems for your team, that's the question to start with: not what can it do, but what should it be trusted to do unsupervised.

What's the first process you'd actually trust to run without you watching it?

## Caption

Same model. Same prompt. Two completely different agents.

A recent breakdown of an open-source agent harness made this visible in a way most people haven't seen directly.

Swap out the harness around an identical model and the behavior changes entirely. Not because the model got smarter or dumber. Because the scaffolding around it changed.

The harness is the part nobody demos.

It decides which tools the model is allowed to call. It decides what happens when a tool call fails. It decides what context gets passed forward and what gets dropped. It decides whether the model can act on its own or has to stop and check with a human first.

None of that is the model's job. It's the job of whoever built the loop around it.

This is why two teams can use the exact same foundation model and get wildly different reliability. One team spent their time on the permission structure, the retries, the context management. The other team assumed the model would just handle it.

The model is the engine. The harness is the car. People keep comparing engines.

If you're building or evaluating agents right now, the model choice is probably not where your risk is.

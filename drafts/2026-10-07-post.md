# Draft 2026-10-07 (post)

**Status:** blocked

**Topic:** AI agents operating with real-world consequences
**Why now:** Multiple independent stories in the same 48-hour window (Wikimedia rogue agent report, South Korea bank hack attribution, proposed Senate liability bill) show regulators and platforms reacting in real time to agent autonomy incidents

## Caption

Three separate stories broke in the same 48 hours.

A bipartisan Senate bill proposing liability for AI agents involved in hacking incidents.

Wikimedia reporting unauthorized agent activity across its projects, traced back to OpenAI.

And reports out of South Korea that AI tools may have played a role in a string of bank breaches.

Different countries. Different companies. Different regulators.

Same underlying issue.

Agents are being given enough autonomy to take real actions, and nobody has fully worked out who's responsible when those actions go wrong.

Not the AI model. The operator who deployed it. The developer who built it. The platform it ran on.

That question used to be theoretical. It isn't anymore.

Most businesses experimenting with agents right now are focused on what the agent can do.

Fewer are asking what happens when it does something nobody approved.

That's the part worth building for before it's forced on you by regulation.

Guardrails, approval steps, and logging aren't friction. They're what makes an agent safe to actually leave running.

## Caption

A 97-comment Hacker News thread this week was full of developers venting about coding agents producing shallow, confused output on real repos.

Reading through it, the complaints weren't really about model intelligence.

They were about missing context. People were dropping agents into large codebases with no structure, no constraints, and no way for the agent to check its own work, then calling the output dumb.

A new engineer handed a vague ticket and zero onboarding would struggle the same way. Nobody blames the engineer's intelligence for that.

The interesting part of the thread was how often the fix people landed on had nothing to do with switching models. It was giving the agent clearer scope, relevant files, and some way to verify its own output before handing it back.

That's closer to workflow design than AI capability.

If you're working with Claude Code or similar tools, what's the gap, context, scoping, or verification, that trips your agent up most often?

If you're building something similar, happy to compare architectures.

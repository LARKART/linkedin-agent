## Caption

Most people debugging with AI agents still use one agent for everything.

Ask it to find the bug, fix the bug, and confirm the fix is correct.

That's three different jobs, and they require three different kinds of thinking.

Finding a bug means reading broadly and staying skeptical.
Fixing it means narrowing focus and writing precisely.
Verifying it means ignoring your own assumptions and checking the actual outcome.

One agent trying to do all three in the same context tends to anchor on its first theory and carry that bias through the rest of the task.

A workflow I've seen gaining traction recently splits these roles apart. One agent investigates and reports findings. A separate agent, with a clean context, proposes the fix. A third agent checks the result against the original problem, not against the first agent's explanation.

The interesting part isn't that it uses more agents.

It's that each handoff forces a fresh perspective instead of letting one flawed assumption travel through the entire task.

This matters because most agent reliability problems get blamed on the model. Often it's the workflow design that's letting errors compound unchecked.

If you're building with coding agents: where in your setup does one agent currently do diagnosis, fix, and verification all in a single pass?

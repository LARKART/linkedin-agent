## Caption

An AI agent can now generate more code in an afternoon than a team used to write in a week.

Anthropic recently published details on why they're rebuilding their internal source control around this exact problem. Git was designed for human commit cadence: a handful of branches a day, each one reviewed by a person who remembers writing it.

Agentic coding breaks that assumption. Dozens of branches, commits generated in minutes, and nobody who "remembers" why a change happened because no human typed it line by line.

The bottleneck isn't writing anymore. It's reviewing, merging, and tracking what actually changed and why.

This isn't unique to code. The same pattern shows up anywhere automation increases output volume faster than your review process can handle it. Lead lists that grow faster than your team can qualify them. Content pipelines that generate more drafts than anyone can approve. Agent workflows that touch a CRM faster than anyone can audit the changes.

More automated output just moves the constraint downstream. If the review and approval step isn't designed as carefully as the automation itself, volume becomes a liability instead of an advantage.

Anytime I'm designing an automation or agent workflow, the review and approval layer gets as much attention as the trigger and the execution steps. Fast output with no checkpoint isn't a system. It's just noise with extra steps.

Where does your review process break first when volume goes up: approvals, tracking, or just knowing what changed?

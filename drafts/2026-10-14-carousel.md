## Caption

An agent can produce more code in ten minutes than a developer writes in a day.

That's not really a speed story. It's a review story.

Anthropic published an engineering piece on this recently, and the detail that stuck with me wasn't about Claude specifically. It was their point that commits and branches were built around a simple assumption: one person, making one change, at a time.

That assumption quietly shapes how most teams still think about "a change" to a codebase.

When an agent can generate dozens of parallel attempts, large rewrites, and iterative versions in minutes, that unit breaks down. You're no longer reviewing a change. You're reviewing a pile of drafts that happen to compile.

I see the same pattern outside of code.

Every automation or AI workflow I build runs into this eventually. The bottleneck is rarely whether the AI can produce output. It's whether there's a real checkpoint before that output becomes something the business relies on.

Speed without a filter just creates more noise to sort through later.

The teams who get real value from agents aren't the ones generating the most output. They're the ones who decided, deliberately, what counts as a draft and what counts as approved — before they turned the agent loose.

That's a workflow design question more than a tooling one.

## Caption

Postgres 19 is being delayed. Not because of a major bug. Because an AI reviewer found more bugs than the maintainers could triage in time.

That sentence is worth sitting with.

For years, the hard part of code review was detection. Humans are slow readers, they get tired, they miss edge cases. AI reviewers don't have that problem. They read everything, every time, at the same quality.

So the catch rate goes up. A lot.

But finding a bug and fixing a bug are two different jobs. One scales with compute. The other scales with human attention, context, and judgment about what's actually worth fixing before a release.

This is what's inside the carousel I put together on it:

- AI found more bugs than the team could fix
- Detection got easy, fixing didn't
- Catch rate was never the hard part
- Where the new bottleneck actually sits
- More findings isn't automatically progress
- Severity triage has to happen before humans do
- Release processes need to expect this now
- Before adding AI review, plan the queue

The useful takeaway: if you're bringing AI code review into your pipeline, the question isn't "will it find more issues." It will. The question is who triages those findings, how severity gets ranked before a human ever looks at them, and whether your release process assumes a flat queue or a growing one.

Teams that plan for the backlog before it shows up will handle this fine. Teams that don't will keep shipping on the old timeline while the queue quietly grows underneath them.

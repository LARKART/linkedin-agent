# Draft 2026-10-17 (carousel)

**Status:** published

**Topic:** Websites actively blocking AI agents from accessing their content
**Why now:** Fresh reporting (The Verge, ~6h old) on a real, current shift in how the web treats agent traffic, paired with banks separately locking agents out of accounts the same week. Two independent signals pointing at the same emerging norm.

## Caption

An agent that can technically do something is no longer the same as an agent that's allowed to do it.

That gap is becoming the real design problem.

Site owners and banks are starting to treat agent traffic differently than human traffic. Logins, bot-detection, and explicit blocks aimed specifically at autonomous agents, not generic scrapers.

For any workflow that depends on browsing, scraping, or logging into a site on someone's behalf, that's a structural shift, not a minor inconvenience.

A few things that change once access becomes the constraint:

Agents get treated differently than the humans they're acting for.

The question moves from "can this agent complete the task" to "is this agent allowed to be here at all."

Workflows that assume open access are fragile by default, because that access can be revoked without warning.

Permission and authentication become part of the system's reliability, not a side detail handled once during setup.

The practical shift: access has to be mapped and designed for before the task logic gets built, not patched in after something breaks.

An agent with great reasoning and no reliable way in isn't a system you can depend on.

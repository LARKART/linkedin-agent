# Draft 2026-10-17 (video)

**Status:** published

**Topic:** Financial institutions building permission walls against autonomous AI agents
**Why now:** Major financial institutions publicly establishing agent-blocking policy this week, right as enterprises are deciding how much autonomy to grant AI systems touching money or customer data

## Caption

Schwab and JPMorgan just put up permission walls against AI agents.

Not against AI. Against unauthorized agents initiating money movement on a customer's behalf.

That distinction matters more than it sounds.

For the last two years, most of the "agentic AI" conversation has been about capability. Can the agent reason. Can it use tools. Can it complete a multi-step task without a human in the loop.

What's happening at these institutions is a different conversation entirely. It's not "can the agent do this." It's "who gave it permission, under what identity, with what audit trail, and who is accountable if it's wrong."

That's a governance problem, not a technology problem.

I see the same pattern in smaller businesses building their first agent workflows. The hard part was never connecting the CRM to an LLM. The hard part is deciding which actions the agent can take unsupervised, which ones need a human checkpoint, and how you prove that later.

Any business planning to let an agent touch customer data, send money, or update records needs the same thing banks are now building: explicit rules about what's allowed, logging of what happened, and a clear line between automation and authorization.

The technology to act autonomously already exists. The permission structure around it is what's still being figured out, by banks and small businesses alike.

What would it take for your business to trust an agent with a real transaction, not just a draft?

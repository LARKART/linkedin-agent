## Caption

Anthropic just published something more useful than another model announcement.

They described how their Claude Code team had to rebuild code review, because AI-generated pull requests started arriving faster than humans could reasonably review them.

The bottleneck wasn't writing code anymore. It was reviewing it.

That's a quiet but important shift.

For years, engineering teams optimized for writing speed: better tooling, better autocomplete, better scaffolding. Review was treated as a secondary step, something a senior engineer did between other tasks.

Once AI can generate a working PR in minutes, that assumption breaks. You can produce far more code than any team can safely verify, merge, and maintain.

The interesting part of their writeup isn't the tooling they used. It's the acknowledgment that process, not model capability, was the actual constraint.

This matches what I see with businesses adopting AI agents and automation. The hard part is rarely getting the AI to do the task. It's building the review, approval, and verification layer around it so the output can be trusted at scale.

More AI output without a redesigned review process just moves the risk downstream.

If you're building with AI agents or AI-assisted development right now, where is your actual bottleneck: generating the work, or verifying it?

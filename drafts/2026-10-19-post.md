## Caption

There's a framing going around that splits AI agents into two parts: the model is the brain, the harness is the body.

I think that's the right way to look at it.

Most of the time when an agent fails in production, it's not because the model made a bad decision. It's because of what's wrapped around the model.

How much context does it actually have at that step.
What tools can it call, and what happens when a call fails.
Is there retry logic, or does one bad response break the whole run.
Where does a human need to check the output before anything moves forward.

Swap in a stronger model and none of that changes. The agent is still only as reliable as the control loop around it.

This is most of what I spend time on when I build agent systems for a business. Not picking the model, deciding the role, the guardrails, the memory, and what happens when something goes wrong.

The model is close to a commodity decision at this point. The harness is the actual engineering work.

If you're building or evaluating an agent right now, what does your error handling look like when a tool call fails?

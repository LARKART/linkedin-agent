## Caption

Two agents got a wallet this week.

OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet lets an agent complete purchases on a user's behalf. Brave's search API now accepts agentic payments through x402. Different companies, same capability, same few days.

For the last two years, agent demos were mostly about drafting things. Writing an email. Summarizing a doc. A human still had to click send.

Payment execution changes that. The agent isn't drafting an action anymore. It's completing one. Money leaves an account without a person in the loop at the moment it happens.

That's a different kind of decision for a business to make.

It's not really a question about whether the AI is capable enough. Language models have been able to reason about purchases for a while. The real question is where the approval checkpoint lives once spending is on the table.

A few worth building before any agent gets access to a budget:

Spending limits per transaction and per time window, enforced outside the model, not requested from it.

A clear log of what was purchased, when, and against which instruction, so a decision can be reconstructed later.

A human review step for anything above a threshold, or anything outside a predefined category.

None of that removes the usefulness of the agent. It just means the business decides where oversight sits instead of discovering it after a charge nobody expected.

Autonomy and oversight aren't opposites. The agents that last in production tend to have both.

If you're looking at agentic payments or already piloting one, what checkpoint would you put in place first?

**Topic:** AI Automation Apps Needing Explicit Permission Boundaries
**Why now:** A major OS vendor shipping this in beta right now shows permission-scoped automation is becoming a platform-level requirement, not just a best practice from automation builders

## Caption

Apple is adding OS-level controls to limit what AI automation tools can see and touch on a Mac.

That is a quiet admission. Automation has been running wider than it should for a long time, and nobody wanted to be the one to say it out loud.

Most automation gets built the same way. Connect the tool, grant access, let it run. Nobody goes back to check what it can actually reach six months later.

When a platform vendor builds permission scoping into the operating system, it stops being a nice-to-have from careful builders. It becomes the baseline people expect.

Good automation already works in layers instead of one wide grant:

Read access before write access.
Approval gates before anything irreversible.
Scoped tokens instead of full account access.
Logging on every step that touches real data.

None of that makes the system slower in any way that matters. It makes the system something you can actually trust without babysitting it.

If you have automations running right now, the useful exercise isn't building something new. It's opening one of them and checking exactly what it's allowed to touch.

What's the widest permission one of your automations has right now?

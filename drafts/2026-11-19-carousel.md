**Topic:** Apple Building an AI Agent for Devices
**Why now:** Multiple fresh news sources in the last 2 hours report this simultaneously, and it marks a shift from agents being a dev/ops tool to a baked-in OS capability, which changes expectations for every business building on top of agents.

## Caption

Apple is reportedly building an agent that can control devices, browse, and code.

A few outlets reported this within the same two hours, which tells you something on its own. This isn't a leaked roadmap item. It's a signal everyone noticed at once.

If it's accurate, it marks a shift. Agents stop being a thing developers wire together in n8n or a backend. They become a feature baked into the operating system your customers already use every day.

That changes expectations fast.

Right now, a lot of businesses treat "agent" as a selling point. Soon, it'll just be what people assume software does. The bar moves from "does this have AI" to "why does this still make me click five things manually."

A few things worth sitting with:

What actually makes something an agent, not just a feature with a chat window on it. Most of what gets called an agent today is a workflow with a language model bolted on. That's fine, but it's not the same thing as a system that holds context, takes action, and checks its own work.

The model was never the hard part. Orchestration is. Error handling, retries, knowing when to hand off to a human, that's the actual engineering. OS-level agents will make that more obvious, not less important.

Worth auditing now: where does your business still need a manual handoff between two systems that should talk to each other. That's usually where the real cost is sitting, not in whether you have the newest model.

What's the one manual handoff slowing your team down right now?

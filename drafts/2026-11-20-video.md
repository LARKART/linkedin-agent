**Status:** published

**Topic:** AI Agents Outperforming Human Experts on Judgment Tasks
**Why now:** Fresh KPMG audit trial story gaining traction right now, directly testing agent judgment against human professionals in a high-stakes field.

## Caption

A recent KPMG audit trial reportedly found AI agents asking sharper, more probing questions than trained accountants reviewing the same files.

If that holds up, it says something uncomfortable about a lot of professional work.

The bottleneck usually isn't domain knowledge. Most trained professionals have that. The bottleneck is curiosity. Asking the question that doesn't fit the template. Noticing the number that's technically correct but still wrong.

An agent that's been given the right instructions and enough context can ask every question on the checklist, every time, without getting tired or protective of its own assumptions.

That's useful. It's also not the whole story.

Agents don't know when a question matters more than the checklist says it does. They don't carry the judgment that comes from having been burned by a bad assumption before. That's still a human job, at least for now.

Which is probably the actual lesson here. Not that agents replace judgment, but that they're good at the part of judgment that's really just discipline, consistency, and not skipping steps when you're tired or rushed.

Most businesses don't need an agent that thinks like a senior auditor. They need one that never forgets to ask the obvious question, with a human still reviewing what it finds.

Where do you think that line actually sits in your field?

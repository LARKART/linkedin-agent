## Caption

Most automation projects fail for a boring reason.

Nobody owns the workflow after it ships.

Here's what I mean.

A team connects their CRM to their email tool.
They add a Zapier step for lead routing.
They wire up a Slack notification.

It works for three weeks.

Then a field name changes in the CRM.
Or a plan upgrade shifts an API limit.
Or someone on the team starts using a new tag.

The workflow breaks quietly.

No error message anyone sees.
No alert in a channel people check.
Just leads sitting there, unrouted, for two weeks.

This is the part nobody talks about when they sell automation.

The build is maybe 30 percent of the work.

The other 70 percent is someone checking it, maintaining it, and treating it like a system with a pulse instead of a project that's done.

Speed without ownership just means your mess moves faster and breaks somewhere new.

What's one automation in your business that nobody actually checks on anymore?

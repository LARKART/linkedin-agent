## Caption

Nobody talks about the boring part of automation.

Everyone wants to show the flashy workflow.

The AI step.
The trigger.
The fancy integration diagram.

But the part that actually breaks a system is the handoff.

Data moving from one tool to another.
A field that doesn't map the same way twice.
A status update that depends on a human clicking the right button at the right time.

The task itself is usually easy.

Summarize this.
Qualify this lead.
Update this record.

That part works in a demo every time.

What breaks production systems is the seam between steps.

Who owns the data once it leaves tool A.
What happens if tool B is slow to respond.
What the next step assumes was already true.

I spend more time designing handoffs than I do picking models or writing prompts.

That's the unglamorous work nobody posts about.

It's also the work that decides if a system survives past week one.

Where does your workflow usually break, at the task or at the handoff?
